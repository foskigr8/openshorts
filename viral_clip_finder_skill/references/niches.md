# Niche Playbooks — Deep Coverage for 8 Viral Niches

Each niche has different dominant viral mechanics. A motivation podcast and a comedy roundtable produce viral clips through completely different patterns. This file gives you the per-niche playbook so you can tune your judgment.

For each niche, you'll find:
- **Dominant patterns** — which of the 15 named frameworks show up most
- **Hook styles** — what openings tend to land
- **Niche-specific anti-patterns** — failure modes unique to the genre
- **What "good" looks like** — concrete example of a top-tier clip
- **Platform considerations** — where this niche's audience lives and what works where

## 1. Podcasts & Long-Form Interviews

**Source material:** 1.5–4 hour conversations between a host and one or more guests. Joe Rogan, Lex Fridman, Diary of a CEO, Tim Ferriss, Chris Williamson, Huberman Lab, Acquired, All-In.

**Dominant patterns:**
- **Vulnerability Reveal** — guests share personal stories they wouldn't on shorter formats
- **Contrarian Frame** — guests take positions against consensus
- **Status Play** — guests comment on other named figures
- **Open Loop** — hosts ask questions that open loops the guest closes
- **Concrete Specificity** — long-form allows for specific stories with details
- **Pop-the-Balloon** — guests puncture common beliefs

**Hook styles that land:**
- Direct contradiction of a popular belief, delivered with calm certainty ("Most people think X. That's actually backwards.")
- A specific number or named scenario ("I'll tell you exactly what happened at Stripe in 2014...")
- Personal confession with stakes ("I almost quit in 2022. Here's what stopped me.")
- Status move on a named figure ("I worked with Elon for three years. Here's what nobody tells you.")

**Niche-specific anti-patterns:**
- **Insider Baseball** — callbacks to previous episodes, recurring segments, host in-jokes
- **Long-windup guest stories** — guests who take 90 seconds to get to the point
- **Host over-talking** — host interrupts or supplements every insight; the guest's actual line is buried
- **Conceptual abstract talk** — guests drift into jargon-heavy conceptual territory without examples
- **Premise depends on prior segment** — the clip only makes sense if you saw the previous 10 minutes

**What "good" looks like:**

> [01:23:14] Guest: "The moment that changed everything for me wasn't a strategy. It was a panic attack. I was 31, I had just raised $20M, and I was in my bathroom floor at 2am unable to breathe. And I realized — I'd built a company I didn't want to run. I was going to be stuck running this thing for a decade. The investors expected it. The employees expected it. My identity was tied to it. And the only way out was to admit I'd been wrong for three years. So I called my board the next morning and told them I was stepping down. Best decision I ever made. Worst six months of my life."

This is Vulnerability Reveal + Concrete Specificity + Stakes Escalation + Open Loop (the loop opens at "panic attack" and closes at "best decision I ever made"). It works cold — no context needed.

**Platform considerations:**
- TikTok: 25–35s ideal. Cut the host's question, start hard on the guest's first compelling sentence.
- Shorts: 30–45s acceptable. Audience tolerates slightly longer setups if the payoff is dense.
- Reels: 20–30s ideal. Lifestyle/motivation guests (Huberman, Hormozi) outperform pure business guests.
- X: 30–60s works. Quote-tweet amplification rewards contrarian takes and status plays.

## 2. Comedy & Entertainment

**Source material:** Standup sets, comedy podcasts (Tiger Belly, Your Mom's House, Convict, Kill Tony), sketch shows, reaction content, commentary channels.

**Dominant patterns:**
- **Pattern Interrupt** — surprise is the engine of comedy
- **Sensory Hook** — laughter cues signal "this is funny, you can laugh"
- **Concrete Specificity** — specific details make jokes land
- **Payoff Density** — jokes per second matters more than anywhere
- **Vulnerability Reveal** — self-deprecating confessions work as comedy

**Hook styles that land:**
- A surprising specific scenario ("So I'm at this restaurant, right, and she pulls out a spreadsheet...")
- A self-deprecating confession with a kicker ("I cried during a Pixar movie. In theaters. With my kids. Who are 17 and 19.")
- An absurd escalation ("And then it got worse.")
- A misdirection ("The worst advice I ever got was from my therapist. She said...")

**Niche-specific anti-patterns:**
- **Punchline Without Setup** — clip starts at the laugh line, viewer has no idea why it's funny
- **In-Joke Cascade** — callbacks to previous bits, recurring characters, host's running gags
- **Sensory hook without content** — starts with laughter but the joke itself is weak
- **Crowd work without context** — comedian responding to an audience member the viewer can't see/hear clearly
- **Punchline that requires visual** — gestures, facial expressions, props
- **Setup-Heavy Payoff-Light** — comedy version: long story, weak punchline

**What "good" looks like:**

> [00:42:18] Guest: "My therapist told me I have an unhealthy relationship with food. I told her she has an unhealthy relationship with my wallet. We're not seeing each other anymore. [LAUGHTER] Anyway, she was right. I eat when I'm stressed, I eat when I'm sad, I eat when I'm happy. Last week I celebrated getting my taxes done by eating an entire rotisserie chicken in the parking lot of a Costco. Just me, the chicken, and my shame. [LAUGHTER] The cashier saw me through the window. She didn't say anything. She just slowly pulled the blinds."

Pattern Interrupt (therapist zinger) + Concrete Specificity (rotisserie chicken, Costco, parking lot) + Vulnerability Reveal (the admission) + Sensory Hook (laughter cues).

**Platform considerations:**
- TikTok: 15–25s ideal. Comedy is the most platform-native content type. Skip the host's question, start at the joke's setup.
- Shorts: 20–35s acceptable. YouTube audiences tolerate slightly slower setups.
- Reels: 15–25s ideal. Relatable comedy (dating, work, family) over-indexes.
- X: 30–45s works for political/social commentary comedy. Quote-tweets amplify hot-take comedy.

## 3. Motivation & Self-Improvement

**Source material:** David Goggins, Andrew Huberman, Alex Hormozi, Chris Williamson, Tony Robbins, Mel Robbins, Steven Bartlett, Ed Mylett, Lewis Howes.

**Dominant patterns:**
- **Pop-the-Balloon** — THE dominant pattern in this niche
- **Stakes Escalation** — small observation → career/life consequence
- **Contrarian Frame** — "everything you've been told about X is wrong"
- **Concrete Specificity** — specific behaviors and consequences
- **Open Loop** — "the reason you're stuck isn't what you think"
- **Share Trigger** — viewers share to signal they're "in" on the reframe
- **Identity Threat** — challenges how the viewer sees themselves

**Hook styles that land:**
- Direct puncture ("You're not busy. You're just bad at prioritizing.")
- Reframe of a common behavior ("Motivation is a myth. Discipline is what you actually have.")
- Specific call-out of a behavior pattern ("If you check your phone within 10 minutes of waking up, you've lost the morning.")
- Contrarian stance ("Hard work is overrated. Strategic laziness is what builds wealth.")

**Niche-specific anti-patterns:**
- **Fake-Deep Quote** — motivation is the most prone niche to this. "Success is a journey" etc.
- **Insight Without Specificity** — abstract platitudes dressed as motivation
- **Beautiful Sentences, Empty Claims** — quotable but empty
- **Authority Without Substance** — "trust me, I've worked with thousands of people"
- **Cringe Vulnerability** — oversharing that produces discomfort rather than inspiration
- **Cold Take Hot Packaging** — mundane advice dressed in viral language

**What "good" looks like:**

> [00:18:42] Guest: "You keep saying you don't have time. Let me push back on that. You watched three hours of Netflix last night. I know because everyone does. You're not lacking time. You're lacking clarity on what to do with it. And the reason you're lacking clarity is that you haven't done the uncomfortable work of figuring out what you actually want. So instead you fill the time with distraction because distraction feels like progress. It isn't. Distraction is just comfortable decay. And in five years, you'll look back and wonder where the time went. It went where you put it. Into the shows. Into the scrolling. Into the comfortable nothing. That's not a time problem. That's a courage problem."

Pop-the-Balloon + Concrete Specificity (Netflix, three hours, five years) + Stakes Escalation (time → five years → courage) + Identity Threat (challenges "I'm busy" identity).

**Platform considerations:**
- TikTok: 20–30s ideal. Cuts should be aggressive — every sentence must advance the argument.
- Shorts: 30–45s acceptable. YouTube motivation audience tolerates longer.
- Reels: 20–30s ideal. Lifestyle-adjacent motivation outperforms pure hustle porn.
- X: 30–60s works. Quote-tweet amplification strong for contrarian reframes.
- LinkedIn: 30–60s works if the motivation is professionally framed. Avoid personal-life motivation.

## 4. Education & Explainer

**Source material:** Veritasium, Mrwhosetheboss, Mark Rober, Kurzgesagt (translated), Andrew Huberman (technical), PolyMatter, Johnny Harris, Wendover.

**Dominant patterns:**
- **Myth Bust** — explicit correction of misconceptions
- **Open Loop** — question raised, answered by end
- **Curiosity Gap** — "you won't believe what actually happens"
- **Tutorial Promise** — direct utility
- **Concrete Specificity** — numbers, examples, demonstrations
- **Contrarian Frame** — "the textbook version is wrong"

**Hook styles that land:**
- Direct myth statement ("Everyone thinks lightning comes from the ground up. Here's why that's wrong.")
- Surprising number ("Your phone screen is dirtier than a toilet seat. By a factor of ten.")
- Counterintuitive promise ("The fastest way to learn a language isn't immersion. It's this 20-minute daily exercise.")
- Specific demonstration setup ("I built this in my garage for $14 to prove something that everyone gets wrong.")

**Niche-specific anti-patterns:**
- **Premise Depends on Visual** — high risk in this niche; lots of clips reference charts, diagrams, demonstrations
- **Insight Without Specificity** — "science shows X" with no specifics
- **Setup-Heavy, Payoff-Light** — long explanation, thin reveal
- **Authority Without Substance** — "studies show" with no study named
- **Cold Take Hot Packaging** — mundane fact dressed as revelation
- **Cliffhanger With No Resolution** — promise of explanation that never arrives

**What "good" looks like:**

> [00:08:15] Host: "Here's something they don't teach you in school. Your brain doesn't store memories — it reconstructs them. Every time you remember something, you're not playing back a recording. You're rebuilding the memory from scratch, and every rebuild introduces small errors. Which means the more you remember something, the less accurate it gets. The clearest memories you have — the ones that feel the most real — are often the most wrong. Psychologists call this the 'misinformation effect,' and it's why eyewitness testimony, which juries trust most, is actually the least reliable evidence we have. The Supreme Court has known this since 1999. Most of them still haven't updated the rules."

Myth Bust + Concrete Specificity (1999, Supreme Court) + Open Loop (opens with paradox, closes with implication) + Stakes Escalation (memory → justice system).

**Platform considerations:**
- TikTok: 20–30s ideal. Educational content thrives here if dense and surprising.
- Shorts: 30–60s ideal. YouTube Shorts educational audience is large and tolerant.
- Reels: 20–30s ideal. Lifestyle-adjacent education (psychology, health, productivity) over-indexes.
- X: 30–60s works. Myth-busts are highly shareable as corrections.

## 5. Lifestyle & Vlog

**Source material:** Day-in-the-life vlogs, transformations, room makeovers, routines, ASMR-adjacent sensory content, Emma Chamberlain, Kinetic sand/unboxing, "What I Eat In A Day."

**Dominant patterns:**
- **Sensory Hook** — visual and audio cues carry the content
- **Tutorial Promise** — "how to" + aesthetic
- **Vulnerability Reveal** — personal moments, emotions
- **Concrete Specificity** — specific products, specific times, specific routines
- **Stakes Escalation** — small moment → bigger life shift

**Hook styles that land:**
- A surprising reveal ("I quit my $200k job to become a baker. Here's what day 47 looked like.")
- A sensory moment (satisfying pour, satisfying click, satisfying transformation)
- A vulnerability admission ("I'm documenting this because I'm scared I'll quit.")
- A specific number/routine ("5am. Cold shower. Two eggs. Journal for 11 minutes. Then this.")

**Niche-specific anti-patterns:**
- **Premise Depends on Visual** — very high risk; vlog clips often make no sense without visuals
- **Setup-Heavy, Payoff-Light** — long routines, thin takeaway
- **Insight Without Specificity** — "live your best life" platitudes
- **In-Joke Cascade** — callbacks to previous vlogs, recurring characters
- **Stale Trend Reference** — using trend sounds or formats that have peaked
- **Cringe Vulnerability** — over-emotional confession with low stakes

**What "good" looks like:**

> [00:14:22] Host: "So today is the day. Day 30. [BREATHES] I can't believe I'm saying this, but I'm gonna keep going. Not because the routine got easier — it didn't. Because something shifted at day 22 that I wasn't expecting. I stopped dreading the 5am alarm. Not because I became a morning person. Because I stopped negotiating with myself every morning. That negotiation — that's what was costing me 40 minutes a day. Once the negotiation was gone, I had 40 extra minutes. And honestly? That's the whole hack. The hack is removing the negotiation."

Vulnerability Reveal + Concrete Specificity (day 30, day 22, 5am, 40 minutes) + Stakes Escalation (routine → 40 minutes → entire mindset shift) + Open Loop (loop opens at "day 30," closes at "the hack is removing the negotiation").

**Platform considerations:**
- TikTok: 15–25s ideal. Aesthetic-driven, music-forward.
- Shorts: 20–35s acceptable. Slightly longer storytelling works.
- Reels: 15–25s ideal. THE platform for lifestyle — aesthetic, transformation, routine content.
- X: minimal audience for pure lifestyle; works if there's a contrarian or business angle.

## 6. Sports & Gaming

**Source material:** Post-game interviews, press conferences, esports casts, stream highlights, trash talk, clutch plays, analysis shows.

**Dominant patterns:**
- **Status Play** — calling out opponents, praising teammates, evaluating coaches
- **Concrete Specificity** — specific plays, specific stats, specific moments
- **Contrarian Frame** — going against consensus takes on players/strategies
- **Pattern Interrupt** — unexpected outcome or reaction
- **Sensory Hook** — crowd reactions, in-game sounds, player emotions
- **Open Loop** — "here's what they did differently in the third quarter"

**Hook styles that land:**
- A specific number/scenario ("With 47 seconds left, down 6, he called a play nobody's run since 1987...")
- A status move ("LeBron doesn't get enough credit for what he did in 2018. Let me explain.")
- A contrarian take ("Everyone's blaming the coach. The coach is the only one who got it right.")
- A behind-the-scenes reveal ("I was in the locker room after that game. Here's what nobody's reporting.")

**Niche-specific anti-patterns:**
- **Insider Baseball** — references to specific plays, players, or seasons only die-hard fans know
- **Premise Depends on Visual** — high risk; lots of sports/gaming clips require seeing the play
- **Setup-Heavy, Payoff-Light** — long analysis, thin takeaway
- **In-Joke Cascade** — recurring segments, host chemistry bits
- **Stale Trend Reference** — referencing last week's news as if it's current

**What "good" looks like:**

> [00:23:14] Analyst: "Everyone's saying the Chiefs won because of Mahomes. Wrong. The Chiefs won because of one decision by Andy Reid at the two-minute warning that nobody's talking about. He went for it on 4th and 3 from his own 42. Every analytics model said punt. Every commentator said punt. Reid went for it. He got the first down by half a football. And that decision — not the throw, not the catch, not the defense — that decision is what won the game. Because if you punt there, you give the ball back to Burrow with 90 seconds and three timeouts. Reid knew that. He did the math nobody else was willing to do. That's not luck. That's a coach who out-thought the room."

Contrarian Frame + Concrete Specificity (Mahomes, Reid, 4th and 3, 42-yard line, two-minute warning, 90 seconds, three timeouts) + Status Play (elevating Reid over consensus) + Open Loop (opens with "everyone's saying," closes with "out-thought the room").

**Platform considerations:**
- TikTok: 20–30s ideal. Sports clips thrive on TikTok if contrarian or behind-the-scenes.
- Shorts: 30–45s ideal. Longer analysis works on YouTube.
- Reels: 15–25s for highlight clips; 30s+ for analysis.
- X: 30–60s ideal. Sports hot takes are X-native; quote-tweet amplification is massive.

## 7. Drama & Controversy

**Source material:** Beef podcasts, callout videos, leaked clips, hot-take commentary, drama channels (Keemstar, DramaAlert, commentary channels like crit1kal, penguinz0 on drama).

**Dominant patterns:**
- **Status Play** — THE dominant pattern; calling out, taking down
- **Contrarian Frame** — going against the consensus on the drama
- **Concrete Specificity** — names, dates, specific tweets/videos
- **Stakes Escalation** — small incident → bigger pattern
- **Identity Threat** — challenging viewers who identify with one side
- **Open Loop** — "here's what really happened"

**Hook styles that land:**
- Direct call-out ("[Name] lied. I have the receipts. Here's what they don't want you to see.")
- Behind-the-scenes reveal ("I was in the room when this happened. The public version is wrong.")
- Contrarian stance ("Everyone's attacking [Name]. They shouldn't be. Here's why.")
- Specific timestamp/screenshot ("At 4:47 in their video, they said something they've now deleted. Let's look.")

**Niche-specific anti-patterns:**
- **Controversy Bait** — high risk; some drama clips cross into harm, defamation, or brand risk
- **Insider Baseball** — references to ongoing feuds only existing fans know
- **Punchline Without Setup** — calling someone out without explaining why
- **Context-Dependent Punchline** — requires seeing previous drama
- **Stale Trend Reference** — drama that's already old news by clip-time
- **In-Joke Cascade** — callback to previous callouts

**What "good" looks like:**

> [00:18:30] Commentator: "I've stayed out of this for two weeks because I wanted to be sure. Now I'm sure. [Name], in their response video, said they 'never directly promoted the course.' That's technically true. They never said 'go buy this course.' But between minutes 12 and 18 of their original video, they showed screenshots of their earnings from the course four separate times. They tagged the course account in the description. They did a Q&A in their Discord about how the course 'changed their mindset.' They never said 'buy it.' They did everything else. That's not direct promotion. That's influencer marketing 101 — and they know exactly what they were doing. The defense is technically correct and entirely dishonest."

Concrete Specificity (two weeks, minutes 12 and 18, four separate times, screenshots, Discord Q&A) + Status Play (taking down the defense) + Contrarian Frame (going against the "technically true" defense) + Open Loop (opens with "I wanted to be sure," closes with "technically correct and entirely dishonest").

**Platform considerations:**
- TikTok: 20–30s ideal. Drama thrives here but is platform-moderrated — keep claims evidence-based.
- Shorts: 30–45s acceptable. YouTube audiences tolerate longer.
- Reels: 15–30s ideal. Instagram drama audience is younger and engaged.
- X: 30–60s ideal. THE platform for drama — quote-tweets amplify takes.

**Brand-risk warning:** Drama clips can drive huge engagement but damage the brand long-term. Use the Controversy Bait anti-pattern aggressively here. Reject clips that:
- Make specific factual claims about named individuals without evidence
- Attack private individuals (not public figures)
- Touch on active lawsuits
- Weigh in on contested criminal cases
- Make claims about mental health, addiction, or trauma of named individuals

## 8. Business & Money

**Source material:** Founder interviews (How I Built This, My First Million, Acquired, All-In, Founder's Journal), finance YouTubers (Graham Stephan, Andrei Jikh, Patrick Boyle), business commentary (Y Combinator, Indie Hackers).

**Dominant patterns:**
- **Concrete Specificity** — numbers are mandatory in this niche
- **Contrarian Frame** — going against consensus business/finance advice
- **Tutorial Promise** — actionable frameworks, specific tactics
- **Status Play** — evaluating founders, VCs, CEOs
- **Open Loop** — "the reason this company failed isn't what you think"
- **Myth Bust** — "the 10,000-hour rule for founders is wrong"
- **Stakes Escalation** — small tactic → big financial outcome

**Hook styles that land:**
- A specific number/outcome ("I turned $14,000 into $2.3M in 18 months. Here's exactly how.")
- A contrarian take on consensus ("Stop networking. Start being useful. The networking advice is wrong.")
- A myth bust ("The 'fail fast' advice is keeping you from succeeding. Here's the data.")
- A specific behind-the-scenes ("I was in the room when [company] raised their Series B. Here's what the deck actually said.")

**Niche-specific anti-patterns:**
- **Insight Without Specificity** — high risk; "work hard and you'll succeed" without numbers
- **Authority Without Substance** — "trust me, I've worked with founders" without specifics
- **Beautiful Sentences, Empty Claims** — quotable but unactionable
- **Echo Chamber Take** — "remote work is good" presented as hot take in 2024
- **Setup-Heavy, Payoff-Light** — long context, thin insight
- **Fake-Deep Quote** — "success is a journey" in business framing
- **Insider Baseball** — niche jargon, specific firm names only VCs know

**What "good" looks like:**

> [00:34:18] Guest: "I'll tell you the exact moment my business turned around. We were at $40k MRR, stuck for 11 months, and I was about to fire two people. Then I did something stupid — I doubled our prices. Overnight. From $99 to $199. No new features. No announcement. Just a price change. I lost 38% of customers in the first week. I cried in my office on a Wednesday. The next month, MRR was $52k. The month after, $61k. We had the same team, the same product, the same traffic. The only thing that changed was we stopped competing on price and started filtering for customers who actually valued what we built. The customers who left were the ones who filed 80% of our support tickets. They were costing us more than they paid. The 62% who stayed? They pay 2x and rarely email us. The lesson isn't 'raise prices.' The lesson is: who you serve matters more than how many you serve."

Concrete Specificity ($40k MRR, 11 months, two people, $99, $199, 38%, $52k, $61k, 80%) + Open Loop (opens with "the exact moment," closes with the lesson) + Stakes Escalation (price change → monthly revenue → business lesson) + Pattern Interrupt (doubling prices when stuck = unexpected move).

**Platform considerations:**
- TikTok: 25–35s ideal. Business advice thrives here if specific and contrarian.
- Shorts: 30–60s ideal. YouTube's business audience is large and tolerant of length.
- Reels: 20–30s ideal. Lifestyle-adjacent business (solopreneur, side hustle) over-indexes.
- X: 30–60s ideal. Business hot takes are X-native; quote-tweet amplification strong.
- LinkedIn: 30–60s ideal. THE platform for B2B and professional business content. Avoid controversy bait.

## Long-form (Tier L) coverage per niche

Every niche has long-form (60–180s) viral moments that don't compress to short-form without losing the arc. Use the Tier L rubric (modified 8-axis + Narrative Arc) for these. Below is the long-form pattern guidance per niche.

### 1. Podcasts & long-form interviews — Tier L

**Dominant Tier L patterns:** Vulnerability Reveal (full arc), Open Loop (extended), Status Play (extended case), Contrarian Frame (full argument)

**Typical Tier L clip:** A guest tells a complete story — the "how I got here" narrative, the founding myth, the failure and recovery. Often 90–150s. The arc is setup (where I was) → tension (what went wrong) → climax (the realization) → resolution (what I did differently).

**Example:** Founder's "I almost killed the company" story — full pivot narrative from decision → fallout → recovery → lesson. 120s. Cuts to short-form Tier S clips at: the decision moment, the crying scene, the lesson. But the full arc only works as Tier L.

### 2. Comedy & entertainment — Tier L

**Dominant Tier L patterns:** Pattern Interrupt (extended setup), Concrete Specificity (cumulative), Sensory Hook (sustained), Payoff Density (layered)

**Typical Tier L clip:** A comedian's full bit — setup, escalation, multiple punchlines, callback. Often 60–90s. The arc is setup (the premise) → escalation (the specifics get weirder) → climax (the punchline) → resolution (the callback or kicker).

**Example:** A 90-second bit about a bad date — the spreadsheet reveal, the column descriptions, the "where you rank" line, the "Costco product" comparison, the score reveal (6.2), the "too eager to pay" reason, and the kicker ("I haven't been on a date in eight months"). The full arc only works as Tier L; cutting to any single punchline loses the cumulative comedy.

### 3. Motivation & self-improvement — Tier L

**Dominant Tier L patterns:** Pop-the-Balloon (extended), Stakes Escalation (full arc), Vulnerability Reveal (personal story), Concrete Specificity (cumulative)

**Typical Tier L clip:** A speaker's complete reframing — the puncture, the reframe, the personal story that proves it, the path forward. Often 90–150s. The arc is setup (the inflated belief) → tension (the reframe that punctures it) → climax (the personal story that proves the reframe) → resolution (what to do instead).

**Example:** The "you're not busy" reframe extended to a full 120s — the puncture, the calendar audit story, the founder's transformation, the actionable takeaway. The full arc gives the viewer both the insight and the proof it works.

### 4. Education & explainer — Tier L

**Dominant Tier L patterns:** Myth Bust (full argument), Open Loop (extended), Tutorial Promise (multi-step), Concrete Specificity (cumulative evidence)

**Typical Tier L clip:** A full explainer — the myth, the evidence, the correction, the implication. Often 90–150s. The arc is setup (what everyone thinks) → tension (why that's wrong) → climax (the actual mechanism) → resolution (what to do with this knowledge).

**Example:** A 120-second explanation of the "10,000 hour rule" myth — Gladwell's claim, Ericsson's actual research, the 50-hour competence finding, the implication for what skills to start. The full arc is necessary because the myth-bust requires evidence to land.

### 5. Lifestyle & vlog — Tier L

**Dominant Tier L patterns:** Vulnerability Reveal (transformation arc), Concrete Specificity (cumulative), Stakes Escalation (personal growth), Sensory Hook (sustained aesthetic)

**Typical Tier L clip:** A day-in-the-life or transformation segment — the before, the process, the after, the reflection. Often 60–120s. The arc is setup (where I started) → tension (the difficulty of the process) → climax (the breakthrough moment) → resolution (what changed).

**Example:** A 90-second "day 30 of my 5am routine" segment — the day 1 struggle, the day 22 shift, the day 30 reflection, the insight about negotiation. Cuts to short-form at the breakthrough moment, but the full arc only works as Tier L.

### 6. Sports & gaming — Tier L

**Dominant Tier L patterns:** Status Play (extended analysis), Concrete Specificity (cumulative evidence), Contrarian Frame (full argument), Open Loop (extended)

**Typical Tier L clip:** A full tactical breakdown — the consensus take, the contrarian position, the evidence, the implication. Often 90–120s. The arc is setup (what everyone's saying) → tension (why that's wrong) → climax (the specific decision or moment that proves it) → resolution (the lesson).

**Example:** A 120-second breakdown of the Andy Reid 4th-down decision — what everyone said, why they're wrong, the specific math, the implication for coaching. The full analysis requires Tier L length.

### 7. Drama & controversy — Tier L

**Dominant Tier L patterns:** Status Play (extended case), Concrete Specificity (cumulative evidence), Contrarian Frame (full defense), Open Loop (extended)

**Typical Tier L clip:** A full case — the claim, the evidence, the response to counterarguments, the implication. Often 90–150s. The arc is setup (the public claim) → tension (the evidence against it) → climax (the specific proof) → resolution (what this means).

**Example:** A 120-second breakdown of a public figure's response video — the claim, the timestamped evidence (minutes 12–18), the specific receipts, the conclusion. The full case requires Tier L; cutting to a single evidence point loses the cumulative case.

**Brand-risk note:** Tier L drama clips carry MORE risk than Tier S, because the extended length means more content to defend. Apply the Controversy Bait anti-pattern more aggressively for Tier L.

### 8. Business & money — Tier L

**Dominant Tier L patterns:** Tutorial Promise (full framework), Contrarian Frame (full argument), Concrete Specificity (cumulative numbers), Open Loop (extended case study)

**Typical Tier L clip:** A full framework or case study — the problem, the contrarian approach, the specific tactics, the result. Often 90–150s. The arc is setup (the conventional approach) → tension (why it fails) → climax (the specific alternative) → resolution (the outcome).

**Example:** A 120-second breakdown of the term sheet lesson — what most founders do, why valuation isn't the most important term, the specific terms that matter, the personal story of the $12M difference. The full framework requires Tier L.

### Tier L niche routing rules

When orienting for a Tier L clip, the niche detection is the same as Tier S. But the patterns you look for are different:
- Tier S patterns favor single-punchline or single-insight clips
- Tier L patterns favor multi-beat arcs that escalate

If the speaker is telling a story, that's Tier L. If the speaker is making a single point, that's Tier S. If the speaker is building a multi-step argument, that's Tier L. If the speaker is delivering a punchline, that's Tier S.

Many Tier L clips contain 2–3 Tier S sub-clips. When you find a Tier L candidate, also identify the Tier S sub-segments — the user may want to publish both the full arc AND the highlight clips.

## Cross-niche routing

When a source material spans multiple niches (e.g. a business podcast with motivational moments, or a sports podcast with comedy bits), pull from each relevant section. Tag each clip with the niche it's targeting — this matters for the platform fit recommendation and the cut instructions.

A podcast like Diary of a CEO might produce:
- 4 clips from the Motivation playbook (guest's personal growth stories)
- 3 clips from the Business playbook (guest's professional insights)
- 2 clips from the Drama playbook (guest's takes on controversies)
- 1 clip from the Education playbook (guest explains a concept)

Each clip should be cut according to the conventions of its target niche, not the niche of the source podcast. The niche of the source determines the patterns you'll find; the niche of the clip determines how you cut it.

## Meta-patterns across all niches

A few universal truths regardless of niche:

1. **Specificity beats abstraction in every niche.** Numbers, names, scenarios. Always.
2. **The first 1.5 seconds determine whether the viewer stays.** Spend disproportionate attention on the cold-open line.
3. **The clip must stand alone.** No callbacks, no required context, no insider references.
4. **The payoff must arrive.** Open loops must close. Promises must be kept.
5. **Density is rewarded.** Pack more signal per second. Cut every unnecessary word.
6. **Share triggers drive reach.** A clip the viewer wants to share will outperform a clip the viewer just watches.
7. **Honest scoring builds trust.** Don't inflate. The skill's value comes from being a reliable filter, not a generous one.
